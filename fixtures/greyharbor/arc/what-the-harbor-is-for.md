# What the harbor is for

> Ilse's arc across season 1. Three waypoints, each with what it means and what
> landing it looks like on screen (D24).

## Arc

- scope: season
- season: 1
- kind: character

## Statement

Ilse Renn keeps Grey Harbor lit, crewed, and on schedule for a shipping lane that
stopped carrying ships four years ago. She has never said why, including to
herself — asked, she gives the power budget.

The arc asks what the harbour is actually for, and it asks it the only way that
costs anything: by making the answer expensive for somebody who isn't Ilse. Every
waypoint is a larger bill, paid by someone else, that she declines to itemise. The
arc lands when she says out loud who the harbour is for, and it lands badly if she
says it before the harbour has taken something from her crew that she cannot give
back.

This is what you re-read when you have forgotten what this arc was. If a scene
makes Ilse defend the harbour in words, it is probably ahead of its waypoint —
this arc is carried by what she spends, not by what she argues.

## Waypoint 1 — The harbor is a job

- description: Ilse treats the silence in the lane as a scheduling problem. The
  roster, the quarterly, the beacon: all of it is maintenance, none of it is a
  question, and she is genuinely not withholding a reason — she has not looked for
  one. The harbour costs the crew nothing yet beyond ordinary work.
- landing criteria: On screen she runs the harbour by the roster, and answers a
  direct question about the empty lane with a duty time or a figure rather than a
  reason. Nobody is worse off at the end of the scene than at the start. If she
  defends the harbour, or if anyone pays for it, the episode has overshot this
  waypoint.

## Waypoint 2 — The harbor is worth spending on

- description: Ilse spends something real to keep the harbour working — a part
  earmarked elsewhere, hours nobody has, or a person's safety — and does not
  account for it. She still would not call it a choice, and the crew has not yet
  called it one either. The cost lands on a named person, not on an abstraction.
- landing criteria: On screen she diverts a resource that was committed to
  something else, and when challenged she answers with the schedule instead of the
  reason. Somebody identifiable is worse off for it by the end of the episode, and
  neither she nor they say so out loud. If she justifies the diversion, or writes
  it down, the episode has landed waypoint 3 by accident.

## Waypoint 3 — The harbor is hers

- description: Ilse admits, to herself or to exactly one other person, that the
  harbour is not for the ships. This is the arc's hinge: not a decision to stop,
  and not an apology — an accounting. The lights stay on afterwards, which is the
  point.
- landing criteria: On screen she says out loud, or does something that only makes
  sense if, no ship is coming — and the harbour keeps running anyway. The line is
  said to one person or to nobody; a speech to the assembled crew is the wrong
  landing for this waypoint and should be a finding.
