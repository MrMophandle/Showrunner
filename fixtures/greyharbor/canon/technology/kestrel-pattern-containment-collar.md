# Kestrel-pattern containment collar

> The station's answer to a hardsuit: eleven minutes of field, worn at the neck,
> and useless on a rack.

## Identity

- standing: core
- status: active
- aliases: collar, Kestrel collar

## Relations

- stowed-at: Grey Harbor Station

## Body

A Kestrel is a hinged ring worn at the base of the neck. Closed, it throws a
containment field around the wearer — close-fitting, faintly visible where it
meets a surface, and audible inboard as a thin hum. It is not a suit. It gives
pressure and it gives air, it does not give warmth or armour or time, and every
rigger on Grey Harbor can tell you exactly how much of it they have left.

Four of them hang on the rack inside the No. 4 lock. Grey Harbor has had four for
eleven years and has never once had five.

## Facts

- A closed Kestrel collar holds a containment field for eleven minutes, then
  drops it with ninety seconds of warning tone.
- An active collar is visible: the field fogs where it meets a surface, and it
  hums loudly enough to hear through a handset.
- A collar protects only the body wearing it, and only while it is closed.
- Grey Harbor's four collars live on the rack inside the No. 4 lock; a collar on
  the rack is a collar nobody is wearing.
