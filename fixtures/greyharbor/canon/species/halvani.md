# Halvani

> Station-born people, four generations off any planet. Thin, cold-tolerant, and
> exactly as killable by vacuum as anyone else.

## Identity

- standing: core
- status: active
- aliases: harbour-born

## Relations

None. `homeworld` is declared by the species category and left off on purpose —
see `_category.md`.

## Body

The Halvani have lived on stations and hulls long enough that the word for
"ground" and the word for "deck" are the same word. Generations of low gravity
made them tall and light-boned; generations of recycled air made them fussy about
it. They run cold — a Halvani is comfortable at fourteen degrees and will complain
at twenty — and they hear pressure changes before instruments do, which is the
sense that keeps station people alive and makes them insufferable about doors.

None of that makes them hardy. A Halvani body is a body: it needs pressure, and it
does not get to negotiate.

## Facts

The physiology facts are the load-bearing ones — they are what the world-rules
check reads when a Halvani is in a scene, and without them a rule about vacuum
catches nobody.

- A Halvani in unprotected vacuum — no hardsuit, no active containment field —
  loses consciousness in about nine seconds and dies inside two minutes.
- A Halvani cannot hold their breath against vacuum; the pressure differential
  empties the lungs whether or not they cooperate.
- Halvani are comfortable between twelve and eighteen degrees and treat anything
  above twenty-two as a fault in the air plant.
- Halvani bones break easily under load; a Halvani working at full gravity wears a
  brace or wears a cast soon after.
- Halvani hear a pressure change before a gauge shows it, which is why station
  crews argue about doors that are technically closed.
