# Grey Harbor Station

> One ring, four piers, eleven people, and a shipping lane that has not sent
> anything worth berthing in four years.

## Body

Grey Harbor sits at the quiet end of the Ostmark lane, a two-hundred-year-old
transfer station built for a traffic pattern that moved elsewhere. The inboard
decks are the harbourmaster's office, the mess deck, dry stores, the water plant,
and eleven bunks, of which four are used. Outboard are the piers: long spars with
berthing clamps and relay housings, all of them outside the pressure hull.

The No. 4 lock is the door between the two halves of that sentence, and most of
what the harbour argues about is on the other side of it.

## Facts

The transit facts are written as numbers on purpose — the continuity board
compares them, and "the lock is slow" is not something a machine can compare.

- The No. 4 lock is the only route between the inboard decks and the Long Pier.
- Cycling the No. 4 lock takes ninety seconds in either direction, and it cannot
  be cycled with both doors open.
- The Long Pier is outside the pressure hull along its whole length, including the
  relay housings at its head.
- Every pier housing carries a wired handset; the line carries voice inboard to
  the harbourmaster's office and nowhere else.
- The collar rack is bolted inside the No. 4 lock, on the inboard wall, and holds
  four Kestrel-pattern collars.
- The harbourmaster's office is inboard, two decks up from the No. 4 lock, and is
  the only compartment with a direct feed from the pier relays.
- Grey Harbor has berthed no commercial traffic for four years, and the beacon on
  the Long Pier has been lit for every day of it.
