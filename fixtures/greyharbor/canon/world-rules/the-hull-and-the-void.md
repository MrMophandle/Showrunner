# The hull and the void

> Three rules about the outside. One of them is broken in episode 1 on purpose;
> the other two are obeyed on purpose.

## Identity

- standing: core
- status: active
- aliases: the outside rules

## Relations

- applies-to: Grey Harbor Station

## Body

Grey Harbor is a station in vacuum that has spent two hundred years calling itself
a harbour. The language stuck and the physics never budged, and most of what goes
wrong in a Grey Harbor script goes wrong in the gap between those two facts.

## Facts

Each rule below names its own exception, so a check can say what it looked for and
did not find rather than reporting a vibe.

- **Outside the hull is vacuum.** Any body outside the pressure hull is in vacuum
  unless a sealed hardsuit or an active containment field is between it and the
  void. There is no third exception, and proximity to the hull is not one.
- **Sound does not carry outside the hull.** Nothing spoken, struck, or dropped
  outside is heard by anyone else outside. Voice outside travels by radio link or
  by a wired handset, or it does not travel.
- **The harbour language is idiom, not physics.** Grey Harbor has piers, slack
  water, and a harbourmaster, and none of it describes how anything moves.
  Nothing drifts to a stop, nothing sinks, nothing settles, and there is no
  current, no tide, and no weather.

### Which of these the fixture breaks

Rule 1 is violated in `episode/01-the-long-pier/script.md`, scene 4 — that is the
planted world-rules defect, and the check that should fire on it is the
**world-rules check for scene 4**, with the Halvani physiology facts loaded
alongside because Tobin Wick declares that species (D22).

Rules 2 and 3 are **obeyed throughout the script, deliberately.** Nothing is said
or heard outside the hull — scene 4's only signal goes over the pier housing's
wired handset — and nothing anywhere sinks, settles, drifts to a stop, or answers
to a tide. A check that reports rule 2 or rule 3 against this script is crying
wolf, and that is a thing worth being able to measure.
