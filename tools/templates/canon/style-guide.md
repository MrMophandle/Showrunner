# Style guide

## Narration

## The core technique
<!-- Q: Is there one move the prose makes over and over — the thing that would be missing if somebody else wrote an episode? Name it, then show it in a single sentence of your own. -->

## Hard staging constraint
<!-- Q: Is there anything the prose may never do as a matter of staging rather than taste — a vantage it may not take, a thing it may not show? Answer "none" if there is none; for Harbor Lights, no scene is ever told from inside the lamp room itself. -->

## The retention contract
<!-- Q: What must the first minute do to keep a viewer? State it as a rule the script gate can refuse a script for breaking. -->

## Rules of voice
<!-- Q: What does the narration never do? One rule per line — the habits you want refused, which the draft agent writes against and the repetition auditor checks. -->

## Cadence
<!-- Q: How do the sentences move — long and even, short and clipped, deliberately varied? Describe the rhythm and name whatever is allowed to break it, because the flow auditor measures a script against this answer. -->

## Character voices
<!-- Q: How does each recurring character sound different on the page? One line per character — lexicon, sentence length, and the thing they would never say — read by the character auditor. -->

## Register sample
