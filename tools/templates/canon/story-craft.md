# Story Craft — Structural Laws

> Distilled from proven frameworks (Harmon's Story Circle, Parker/Stone's
> But-Therefore rule, Swain's scene/sequel, Chekhov's ledger, the TV button).
> Loaded by the outline stage and enforced by the structure auditor. Sister
> file to `episode-formula.md` (beats) and `style-guide.md` (voice); this file
> is about CAUSALITY and SHAPE. It is a house default: keep it, edit it, or
> replace it with your own craft rules.

## The causality law — BUT / THEREFORE, never AND-THEN
Every beat connects to the previous one with **BUT** (a turn) or **THEREFORE**
(a consequence). If two adjacent beats connect with "and then," one of them is
dead weight — cut it or give it teeth. An outline must read as a causal chain:
*she takes the contract, THEREFORE she crosses to the light, BUT the lamp is
already burning, THEREFORE…* This is the single best test of whether a story is
moving or merely continuing.

## The circle (mapped onto the beat formula)
1. **Comfort** — the characters in their known world (grounding)
2. **Need/Want** — what they are after, and why today (the hook)
3. **Cross the threshold** — leaving the known world for the one that costs
4. **The road of trials** — complications escalate (each by BUT/THEREFORE)
5. **Get what they came for** — the find, the answer, the thing itself
6. **Pay the price** — the cost lands (the crisis; this is where death lives)
7. **Return** — the way back; changed people, changed cargo
8. **Changed** — the world looks the same; the characters (or the audience)
   know it isn't
Steps 7–8 are NOT optional. Skipping the return-and-changed beats is how an
episode ends "almost mid-paragraph" and leaves the listener with *so what?*

## Scenes: enter late, leave early — and pay the sequel tax
- Enter each scene at the last possible moment; leave at the first.
- **Scene** = someone wants something, meets resistance, and the situation TURNS.
  A scene that ends where it started isn't a scene.
- **Sequel** = after a hard turn (a death, a reveal), the story owes a REACTION
  beat: characters absorb, disagree, decide. Grief that is never processed on
  screen never happened for the listener. The sequel is where feelings live —
  budget real minutes for it after any grief-register event.

## Setup and payoff — Chekhov's ledger
- Everything that FIRES was PLANTED; everything PLANTED either fires this
  episode or is deliberately banked as a series seed (logged in the outline's
  `## New canon proposed` — see `series-arc.md`'s pacing rule for how much of
  the arc one episode may spend).
- Nothing fires unplanted: a rescue, a tool, a skill, or a betrayal that
  appears in beat 6 must exist by beat 3.
- The outline must be auditable this way: every payoff names its plant.

## Endings — the "so what?" test
An episode may not merely stop. The denouement owes FIVE duties, in roughly
this order, and the outline must name how each is paid:
1. **Resolve the job** — the obligation that opened the episode closes: paid,
   failed, or transformed. Say what happened to the thing everyone wanted.
2. **Land the emotional note ON A CHARACTER** — not on an event or a wide
   shot. Someone carries the episode's weight in a specific, concrete moment
   (a name written in a log; a pair of hands going still; an empty seat).
3. **Restate the world's indifference** — the place keeps turning and does not
   notice what it cost. Cut this duty if your show's signature is the opposite,
   but decide deliberately rather than by omission.
4. **Plant or water exactly one seed** — the forward hook, quiet, unexplained
   (a light that should be dark; a bulletin nobody connects; a name in a
   ledger).
5. **The final button** — the last line lands on a quiet, cold, SPECIFIC
   image or line, preferably in a character's voice or on their action.
   Never a summary, never a moral, never mid-motion.
The test: imagine the listener at the last second. If their thought is
*so what?*, duties 2 and 5 were skipped. If it's *oh no* or a slow exhale,
the ending landed.

## Openings (for completeness)
Cold opens pitch DREAD or CURIOSITY, never unearned grief (see
`episode-formula.md`'s "Death rules"). The open must pose the question the
episode answers — and the ending's duty 1 must answer it.

## What the structure auditor checks (summary)
Causality chain (no and-thens) · circle steps 7–8 present · sequels after
hard turns · every payoff planted, every plant resolved-or-banked · all five
ending duties paid · the final button is quiet, cold, specific, on-character.
